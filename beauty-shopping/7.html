The Concept

No. 07 / Editorial

"Monochrome isn't the absence of colour — it's colour with the volume turned all the way up on contrast instead of hue."

This look strips the palette down to a single greyscale line and asks every product to earn its place through depth, not pigment. Skin is set to a flat matte ash finish with zero shimmer, so light reads as shadow and structure rather than shine.

The eye is built in three steps of the same tone — a pale silver wash, a mid-grey crease, and a near-black smoked outer corner — diffused into one continuous gradient. Brows are kept soft and undefined, lashes are left natural, and the lip closes the look in a bare neutral that disappears against the skin.

Designed for camera, runway, and black-and-white portraiture, where colour would only compete with line.

How It's Built

Step-by-step / 4 stages
01
Flatten the canvas
Skin is primed and set with the ash matte powder before any colour goes on, so no later step picks up unwanted shine.
02
Wash the lid in silver
The lightest tone from the graphite palette is pressed across the lid and just under the lower lash line to set the base value.
03
Smoke the crease and corner
Mid-grey builds the crease, near-black is packed only into the outer corner and diffused inward — never touching the inner third of the eye.
04
Close on bare lip
A single layer of the neutral tint, blotted rather than blended, keeps the mouth quiet so the eye reads as the only statement on the face.
